GGC: 3 Months In

The Harbour and the Lighthouse

In one of the sensemaking sessions, Nadim offered a metaphor that's been resonating. You can find the full framing in the Q2 10x100 quarterly deck.

"He described the collective as a harbour — a place that makes it easier for people to take boats out (fast ones, slow ones, small ones), explore, come back to share what they've learned, and then decide what to change from the original crew and tools before going out again. Partners and others who aren't part of Dm can also dock and learn with us. The GGC is the harbour work. And maybe we're missing a lighthouse?"

— Nadim, at the May sensemaking session

The mission sensemaking work — the strategy sessions, the 10x100 — is the lighthouse work. Knowing where we're heading, what we're orienting around, the golden threads we want to keep.

June Dispatches

Sun, Screens, and Sea Level Rise ☀️

Summer is arriving. Long evenings, iced coffee, and a strange collective urge to finally close that browser tab that's been open since February.

Here's how to lean into the season — while staying sharp on what the heat actually means.

🌊

OOO with intention

Heading away in June or July? Set a proper OOO, flag cover in your project channels, and log time before you leave. Future-you will be grateful. So will Andrea.

📚

Summer reading drop

Share what you're reading this summer in #org-hq. Last year's thread surfaced some gems. Books, essays, long reads — all welcome. Use #addtonewsletter and we'll compile a list in Issue #4.

🤝

Summer rendezvous

If you're crossing paths with a colleague this summer — a coffee, a walk, a conference — flag it in #org-hq with a photo. We love documenting the human fabric of Dm in these pages.

🌡️ A note on the heat: As you apply your SPF this summer, our Madrid team is mapping heat resilience strategies for one of Europe's most at-risk cities. Meanwhile, our RiskSense team just ran their first Risk Clinic in Mumbai — 17 participants from across the city, mapping how heat and flooding cascade through everyday life. Siddhi's read from the room: communities there aren't navigating resilience conditions, they're navigating survival conditions — creating options in real time with severely constrained choices, deep mutual aid dependency, and compounding shocks that get harder to recover from each time.

This is the pattern across the global south: the places that contributed least to the climate crisis absorb the worst of it, first. Mumbai, Nagpur, coastal communities in Ratnagiri — not abstractions in a risk framework, but people whose routines are already structured around infrastructure failure. The heatwaves arriving earlier in European summers are, for much of the world, already the baseline. It's okay to enjoy the sunshine. It's also okay to let that sit with you.
