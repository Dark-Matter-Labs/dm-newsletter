GGC Experiment #7

Four publishing guides are now finalised

The Peer Review and Dm Standards team have now finalised the guides (Final V5) on how we write and publish at Dm. There are now four: attribution, publishing, AI usage, and a new style and standards guide. They're a shared reference, not a rulebook — and Gurden has turned them into a Claude skill, so you can run a draft through it for feedback before publishing.

  • Attribution guide — how to credit and license work, with templates, plus an appendix for websites and digital products. Open guide
  • Publishing guide — peer review guidelines, where and how to publish blogs and posts, and a pre-publication checklist. Open guide
  • AI usage guide — which AI tools we have subscriptions for, and how to review work that used them. Open guide
  • Style and standards guide — how we write for a real reader: anchoring complex ideas, coining terms only when they earn it, and being clear about where we write from. Open guide

Questions go to #help-publishing. Ana, Gurden, Eunsoo, Calvin, Prateek and Emma can help.

Team: Eunsoo, Calvin, Fang, Ana, Gurden, Prateek, Emma  ·  Claude skill: Gurden
GGC Experiment #4

A shared language for how we work together

Just out at the start of July: we shared first versions of three documents that have been in the works for months, giving us a common language for how people engage at Dm.

  • Forms of Engagement — what it means to be a Dm Contributor, Member, Steward, Pathway Partner or Fellow, including mutual commitments, benefits, and how transitions and contracts work.
  • Archetypes — a shared language for the main ways people contribute: Producer, Project Holder, Pathway Builder, Mission Holder, Steward, OrgDev.
  • Commitments — our social contract as a set of commitments: what we ask of each other at the individual, relational and collective levels, organised by principle and role.

There's an interactive guide that lets you explore all three together, filter by your role, and run a short questionnaire to see the parts most relevant to you. This is Version 1 — the team wants to hold it stable long enough to test it in practice, but it's not closed: you can leave comments directly in the source documents. GGC sessions to talk it through are happening this week.

Team: Sophia, Iyana, Jason, Eunsoo, Fang

Lund 2035: what we're learning about visioning

Meggan published an honest reflection on the Lund work, Visioning without execution is just expensive dreaming. It's written for cities and others doing visioning and workshop work, drawing on the ‘What if… Lund 2035’ exhibition the team built with Lund municipality — six evidence-based scenarios for a climate-neutral city, each grounded in real municipal data and the cost of inaction.

A few of the lessons that travel beyond Lund: assumptions are almost always wrong until you actually look (the team's hunches on retrofit costs, heat islands and solar all shifted once they checked the data); cities often present data more optimistically than the detail supports; and siloed departments are baked into the structure, not just a local quirk — some of the most useful work is simply surfacing the data and expertise an organisation already holds but hasn't connected. The piece argues that a vision only matters if there's a credible path to executing it.

Writing: Meggan  ·  Team: Maria, Anette, Alex H

Seasonal note

Summer, and the heat we're working on ☀️

A lot of the team is heading into peak summer now: long evenings, OOO messages, a quieter inbox. The heat itself is still central to the work. Malik summed it up this month, posting from an event on ‘making heat visible’.

It runs through a few of our projects: the Madrid heat-resilience work in Europe, and the RiskSense Risk Clinics in Mumbai and across India, where people are dealing with survival conditions, not just resilience. The places that did least to cause the climate crisis keep taking the worst of it. Emma's note this month, on a community-led heat solutions compendium and passive-ventilation brick housing in Tanzania, is a good reminder of how much grounded local innovation is already happening.
