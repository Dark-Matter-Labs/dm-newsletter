Spin-in

RZ Studio: Dm’s first spin-in

On 23 September Leon shared the news: RZ Studio Ltd was officially incorporated in August as a company limited by guarantee in the Dark Matter Labs Group. Its directors are Jen Hooke, Indy, and Leon, with Thao working alongside them on the studio work.

RZ works with place-based partners to build, and then allocate anchor investment into, local infrastructure that lets capital flow into bioregional work on different terms. Two of the three first-cohort partners are already underway: Moray Firth Bioregion in Scotland, the Orne Watershed in France, and South Yorkshire Bioregion in England. The aim, in Leon’s words, is to prove that a different investment paradigm is possible.

Around £3m is on its way from the Thirty Percy Foundation, and RZ is raising a further £7m to build the team and run cohorts two and three in 2027.

There is an updated concept note for anyone who wants more than the website offers, particularly funders. Joshua pointed out it is relevant to urban resilience work, and Adam and Leon flagged a link to the Cornerstone work in Sheffield and to emerging work in South Yorkshire with Indy and Vlad.

Really excited for RZ to have fully kicked into gear after such a long incubation period and also for Dm to experiment with spin-ins in a really practical way.

— Leon
Directors: Jen Hooke, Indy, Leon  ·  Studio: Thao  ·  Funding: Thirty Percy Foundation
Change orchestration

Cycle two is underway

Cycle two of the change orchestration experiments runs until the end of November. Eunsoo met most of the experiment leads in September and posted a summary of what each of the seventeen experiments will do, with a longer write-up in Notion. The parts most likely to touch your week:

  • Time reporting: the target is 90% logged every month by the end of November, so that we see notable improvements in our ability to recognise and forecast income and thus manage our finances together. Log by day three of the following month, and make sure every project has a secondary holder who can cover project completion.
  • Pathway time: there will be one Streamtime job with an entity drop-down. If you logged pathway time this year, Jason or Louise may ask you for a rough split by entity.
  • Design and AI standards: Anahat is leading work on templates, a single brand baseline, and design guidelines that AI tools can follow.
  • Capital: a new core-funding proposition and a review of the Growth Pot are in progress, with business model sessions run by Malik.
  • Dm Uploads are coming back. Anyone can propose a session to share work in progress.

Telling Health Keepers what you are noticing

Health Keepers, the nine Stewards who look after organisational health as a whole, meet every two weeks. They noticed they had no reliable way of hearing about issues before those issues fell through the cracks, so Eunsoo set up a Dm Signals form as a two-and-a-half-month trial. It asks five optional questions: what is on your mind, what you are noticing inside Dm, what you are seeing outside it, what might become a problem in three months, and an emoji for how your week has felt.

Eunsoo turns the responses into a synthesis every fortnight, Health Keepers review it, and decisions that they take as a result are logged. If something is urgent, do not wait for the form: message a Health Keeper or Eunsoo directly. The current Health Keepers are Sophia, Annette, Adam, Andrea, Jorn, Leon, Prateek, Eunsoo, and Jason.

The form

form.typeform.com/to/qUjAAXiJ. It takes a couple of minutes. The emoji question feeds an anonymous mood check for future issues of the Dispatch.

Signal Snapshot (22–28 September 2026)

Read the full synthesis. Seven of you shared signals (six from Dm Stewards). The overall picture is of a team that broadly trusts the direction of travel but is stretched. Many of you see the change orchestration work, the new Stewards group, and shared financial stewardship as progress, and the Mission Pathways pop-up as a good moment to make sense of things together. Outside Dm, we are still highly regarded — though some find us dense, and partners are curious about how we deliver. The strain sits in two places: urgent work landing on the same few people as new working groups form, and uncertainty about funding and how xCO and DML relate to each other. Several of you also felt the Gathering’s cancellation more than expected, and asked for more deliberate acknowledgement of each other’s work while remote. Health Keepers will discuss the xCO–DML question, the load on working groups, and how to hear from those who are less visible. If you didn’t respond this time, we’d especially like to hear from you next round.

Berlin

From 8 to 10 October, colleagues from Seoul, Madrid, London, and elsewhere spent working days at medulla. Robyn put the week together: Civic Ledger working sessions, a morning tour of xCO options and positions (recorded for anyone not in Berlin), co-working, and shared lunches with the wider medulla community.   • Friday 9 October: Governing Together’s first birthday, a potluck hosted by Gyorgyi with contributions from Anahat and others.

Berlin 2050° followed on from the Berlin Heat Risk Clinic, held on 19 September as part of the Public Vitality Camp, which nextLearning brought to medulla for the day. Civic Ledger moves from experiment to implementation

Eunji introduced the Civic Ledger team for those who have not met them yet. Eden leads the app backend design, Kai supports the micro-experiments, and Sungwon is the Product Owner, bridging strategic design, development, and the city pilots.

The Korean app is heading to local marketplaces and app stores, and moving from experiment to implementation is surfacing a lot of hidden regulatory requirements around location data and payment gateways. The team has also started sharing an archive of the writing, research, and function and tool catalogues from the past nine months, with a smaller group first.

They have decided not to apply to the 2027–29 DUT Open Call. Instead they will spend the next three months on fundraising, communications, and conversations that take the early pilot evidence into new domains.

Lead: Eunji  ·  Team: Koo, Eden, Kai, Sungwon, Fang  ·  Thanks to Amelia and Alex for the open discussion on DUT

City Transitions Lab: three portfolios, one room

On 24 September, Joshua and Sophia ran a cross-pollination session for the City Transitions Lab’s three portfolios: Urban Resilience (Maria, Meggan), Resilient Food Systems (Mike, Anette), and Just Economic Transitions (Tom, Marcial). Each team had a one-pager to read beforehand, and the brief was to look for where the pieces could strengthen each other as the lab takes shape beyond Net Zero Cities.

Maria has since pulled together the portfolio mapping method she has been developing, with the learnings, city cases, and testimonials, plus a simple interactive mapping tool for cities that do not want to start in Miro. She also turned the Lund portfolio map, co-created by 50 people, into an interactive version where you can trace the full chain of connections. She is asking for input on what to do next, and for help publishing it. The links are internal for now.

Nadim, Saem, and Gurden built the NZC Intelligence Engine that the cities team is now using, which Maria thanked them for in the CTL recap.

CTL session: Joshua, Sophia  ·  Portfolio mapping: Maria, with Simon, Eunsoo, Joshua, Anette, Tom, Alex, Joost, Meggan, and the cities team

Launch of the Unfurling Collective

After years of contributions and condition-building from Annette and Indy alongside many peers in our field, the Unfurling Collective is now confirmed and Dm is one of the founding members: a UK-based network to build strategic muscle in the field of Alternative Futures, with an initial £8m in investment from JRF, governed via a many-to-many approach. The public launch will happen in the New Year. The final trustee pass was confirmed in early October, and money is due to move to the fiscal host in December.

With: Annette, Indy  ·  Earlier groundwork with JRF: Emma

Power Project UK

Power Project UK is live as one of xCO’s positions. In September the team worked through the legal and financial structure for a peer-led process to resource next-generation civic power organisations, and in Berlin Fang ran a session on how Civic Ledger links to the Power Project work in the UK.

People

Comings and goings

Theo is moving on

Theo shared on 6 October that he will not be coming back after his paternity leave. He is joining La DIHAL, the French government department that implements housing first policy nationally, as a product owner on an app that aims to reduce evictions from rent arrears by identifying households earlier and intervening sooner. He will be working out of the Grande Arche de la Défense.

He wrote openly about how he came to Dm four years ago: he had lost his job as a social worker after reporting working conditions to the service’s funders, he knew of Dm from Indy’s talks in Adelaide, and he applied on a whim through the Dm Discord. He thanked Gurden, Romy, and Axel for believing in him at the start.

Something about Dm’s work on systems change and my experience working in broken systems clicked and meant I was able to be honest about my experiences and motivations.

— Theo

The thread filled up fast. Eunsoo wrote that Theo had helped shape the culture and norms around how Dm thinks about and builds technology, and that the civic tech calls will not be the same. Andrea thanked him for being a partner in crime on transfer pricing. Ana said that helping to prevent evictions feels especially important seen from Madrid right now. He is staying in the alumni channels, and his contact details are in his post if you are ever in Paris. Thank you, Theo.
