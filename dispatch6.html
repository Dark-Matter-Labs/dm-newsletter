Three days in the UK

The Mission Pathways pop-up

In the last week of August, everyone at Dm with a pathways-building role met in the UK for a three-day pop-up: twenty-four active pathway builders, working on more distributed pathways-building, stronger relationships and influence in our partnerships, and more relevance, mutual support, and coordination across the work. Annette shared the pre-read deck in advance, and it is worth reading whether or not you were there.

The pre-read pulls together what already exists in the system, gathered mostly from 10x100 and mission sensemaking conversations: our aims and ambitions, the golden threads running through the work, the seasons we have been through and the trajectory we are on, how we do strategy, the craft of pathways building, where demand is emerging, and Dm-level fundraising plans. Some sections are well established and some are early ideas, and comments can go straight on the deck.

At the last 10x100, Max talked about creating deeper and wider pathways through strategic political moves and tactical approaches. Michelle picked up the pop-up's focus on relationality and content-driven future planning.

Gurden and Eunsoo recorded and transcribed the sessions and had Claude build an AI-generated synthesis site, readable in quick or full detail mode. It went up fast, and the team is open about the tension between AI-assisted synthesis and human harvesting, so corrections and feedback are genuinely wanted.

Governance

The first Dm Steward constellation 🌱

On 13 August, Sophia announced the first Dm Steward constellation, closing a process that has been running since April. It drew 39 nominations, including nine self-nominations, across 33 submissions.

The themes that came through the nominations were about caring for the whole, distributing responsibility more broadly, bringing relational maturity and power-awareness to stewardship, and making it sustainable rather than creating another layer of concentrated responsibility.

How it is organised

The constellation sits across three interconnected circles, with some Stewards in more than one, which gives the circles connective tissue rather than hard edges:

  • Mission & Pathways
  • Org Design & Structures
  • Capability & People

Health Keepers stay responsible for whole-organisation strategic health, risk, and inflection-point decisions, with the Stewards working closely alongside them to sense and act on what is emerging across Dm.

Seventeen people have stepped in: Alicia, Alizé, Andrea, Annette, Adam, Eunji, Eunsoo, Fang, Gurden, Indy, Jason, Joost, Jorn, Leon, Michelle, Prateek, and Sophia.

The intention isn't to arrive with fully formed mandates and processes, but to co-create them through the experiment: where does the system most need stewardship, what responsibilities should sit where, how do we best use our collective capacity, and what conditions do Stewards need to hold this well?

— Sophia

It runs as a six-month experiment with a review at three months and again at the end, looking at whether these are the right circles and mandates, how well signals move across the system, and what capacity stewardship actually needs to be sustainable. The Stewards have asked to be reached out to, so do.

Announced by: Sophia  ·  On behalf of: the People team and Health Keepers  ·  Working group: Sophia, Jorn, Annette, Eunsoo
Change orchestration

The next cycle of hunches and experiments

The GGC has formally disbanded, and the work now continues as change orchestration on a twice-yearly cycle. Annette shared the outline for the next cycle on 14 August while Eunsoo was on leave, and Eunsoo has since updated the deck with detail on each experiment and who is holding it.

Much of this cycle is about grounding and re-onboarding ourselves in the new governing systems, then building out our learning, distributed pathways, and strategy muscles. The experiments span, among other things:

  • Team re-onboarding to the new systems
  • Embedding what was designed last cycle, including the new Stewards in their roles
  • Talent strategy and forecasting, and offboarding practices
  • Updated plans for raising pooled funds and engaging other revenue sources
  • AI and knowledge management infrastructure
  • Design, comms and AI standards
  • Creating a headline view of our mission, strategy and core assets
  • Sandbox conditions for work in a focus phase
  • Protocols for entity changes and spin-outs

Annette was straight about what this will feel like: for some people the changes will be big and ask for a fundamental shift in how they think about their role, and for others it will be small adjustments to daily habits. Some things need everyone aligned, like the standards systems and mandatory data reporting. Cumulatively they expect a notable effect on our culture.

There is a slide with your name on it

The deck has a slide listing the support each experiment needs, with blank cells to offer help. A second Q&A on the hunches and experiments runs in September. The first was on 19 August, co-led by Eunsoo, Sophia, and Annette.

What the data says

Baseline survey: an upward trend, and a clear next problem

Eunsoo shared the results and analysis of the second baseline survey on 20 August. Comparing it against March gives us the first real measure of whether the experiments changed anything. Overall the trend is up, including confidence in the change orchestration process itself.

Where we improved most

  • More people feel they have legitimate channels to raise concerns about publishing.
  • More people feel organisational risk is shared fairly, relative to their level of engagement.
  • More people understand what it means to be a Dm Member.
  • Fewer people are affected by unresolved interpersonal conflicts.
  • More people know who should handle an unexpected organisational issue, and how.

Where we are still weak

  • Being aware of colleagues' work and feeling connected to the whole
  • Knowledge and learning flows
  • Anticipating talent and upskilling needs
  • Mission coherence
  • Coordination across teams

Annette's summary from the Q&A: the governance-focused experiments have put the structures in place to start dealing with things in a more coordinated way. Mission coherence, learning, and coordination are the harder, entangled problems, and they are the focus of the next cycle.

The Change Orchestration website now carries the detailed analysis, including anonymised stats on who responded. It is the one-stop place to see what is happening in the experiments, with #org-governance-transition for questions, feedback, and updates.

Quarterly

The 10x100, and what came out of the breakouts

Yesterday's quarterly ran with Robyn hosting while Joshua was unwell, and Adam on the technical side. It had been moved from 19 August to sit after the Mission Pathways pop-up, and that earlier slot was used for the Stewards and hunches Q&A instead.

After the organisational updates, the session split into four breakout rooms, one per piece of work:

  • Lund radical scenarios — Anette, Maria and Meggan on the City of Lund collaboration, where the team mapped and visualised the climate action plan, combined the different departments, and built scenarios with concrete numbers to show what different choices actually produce.
  • Risk Intelligence Lab — Sofia and Prateek on the shift from initiative to lab, and the open question of how to hold actuarial and qualitative risk assessment together.
  • Camden food systems — Aleksander on coordinating purchasing power to build more resilient supply chains and localised food systems.
  • Civic Ledger — Fang and Eunji on the Berlin learnings, and where the work goes next.

Indy pulled out the questions that ran across all four: how we handle strategic branding, what economic function each piece of work is performing, and whether our theories of change actually line up across the organisation.

Feedback taken

The clear message from the room was that the breakouts were too short, and 10 minutes longer would have been better. The next one is on 18 November, and Robyn, Adam, and Joshua are working out a tag-team hosting model among the team, so let them know if you are interested in co-hosting a future 10x100.

A hard call

The October gathering is postponed

On 14 August, Adam shared a recommendation from the Health Keepers to delay the October all-team gathering to at least late Q1 next year.

The reasoning was laid out: reserves are depleted in some parts of the organisation, our pathways and the continuity of work are not as strong as we would like, and a good number of people had flagged clashes between the gathering dates and other events that matter for developing the work. Meanwhile the smaller, highly specific pop-ups have been generating real energy, so that is where the focus goes for now.

Ultimately we could not justify with integrity Dm making this significant investment of all our shared time and resources on an all team Gathering given this picture.

— Adam, for the Health Keepers

The note also acknowledged what this costs, because for a lot of us the gathering is the thing we get most out of all year. Responses in the thread were a mix of disappointment and agreement. Maria said she felt genuinely sad. Anette called it sad but wise, and said she would see everyone in spring. Ivana admitted the timing was easier alongside autumn school holidays. Amelia thanked Adam for making a hard call early and communicating it clearly.

Work towards a gathering early next year is starting, and support for pop-ups continues where they are needed. Adam is also picking up the wider question of where we invest energy in gathering at all, given how well the small intentional formats have been working.

Recommendation from: the Health Keepers  ·  Shared by: Adam

What we're building

Civic Ledger: what Berlin taught us

Fang, Eunji, and Koo have published a page capturing the key learnings from the Civic Ledger Berlin week. It covers the shift towards broader agreement infrastructure, how distributed actors, responsibilities, and resources might be composed into consequential action, fuzzy ruling, meta-design, and the team's emerging thinking on lead markets and the civic tech economy. There are demo videos too.

They have asked a specific question of the team, which is worth answering: what feels strongest, what is unclear, and what is worth pushing further.

Camden becomes London-wide

The Camden food mission has moved fast since the August dinner and workshop. The team is now working with Sustain on separate but linked bids, focused London-wide with Camden at the centre. Adam has been securing Oxford and the Oxford policy briefing as partners, and a set of prototype tools has been built out to carry the portfolio: a horizon navigator, a Camden map, a nutrition cascade model, and a nutrition supply map. Gurden is moving the hosting onto Dm's own server so the team can make changes without waiting on anyone.
