Launch

Civic Ledger is live 🎉

On 23 July, Fang announced the launch of Civic Ledger, the next phase of the Permissioning the City work. You can see it at civicledger.city.

Civic Ledger helps cities move past rigid yes-or-no permission systems. It lets residents, stewards, and public servants make conditional, transparent, and accountable agreements about how shared and underused urban assets get used, which opens room to imagine better ways of living together and builds the collective power to act on them.

This one has been years in the making. Eunji noted afterwards that a lot of the work behind it is not always visible across the wider team: Eden and Kai holding the engineering together while meeting administrative requirements that can pull time away from the actual innovation, Ahyoung developing the narratives and positionality, and Doeun carrying a lot of the administrative load.

New lab

Introducing the Risk Intelligence Lab

Sofia announced on 15 July that we are formalising a new lab: the Risk Intelligence Lab. It builds on the sensing, mapping, and data work that has been growing inside the RiskSense initiative and earlier projects, and it repurposes what was the Sensing & Mapping Studio. It sits alongside our other labs (Beyond the Rules, Capital Systems, Next Economics, Property & Beyond).

Practically, RiskSense now runs as two connected registers. The Risk Intelligence Lab is the internal one, holding experimentation, learning, and the hypothesis log, led by Sofia. RiskSense stays as the public register for brand, portfolio, and partnerships, led by Prateek.

If you are on a project or in an arc and want to explore a risk dimension, talk to the lab. That could mean a peer exchange session, help finding the right person, or a proper project-based collaboration. The revised operating model has the full breakdown, and #lab-risk-intelligence is the place to ask.

Lab lead: Sofia  ·  Supported by: Prateek, Eunsoo, and the RiskSense team
Change orchestration

The second baseline survey

The second baseline survey is open until early August. The first one in March got 28 responses, and comparing the two is how we measure whether anything has actually shifted during the experiment phase. It takes about five minutes. Joshua reported it was a nice reflective process and took him ten.

One thing to watch: you need to press Submit at the end, or the response is only recorded as partial. Results will be published in early August, before the next cycle of experiments begins.

Portfolio

An honest read on the Dm matrix

Since June, Eunsoo and Jason have been going through every arc, lab, and studio on the Dm matrix one by one, talking to whoever holds it, or working out who does, to get a current read on what is active, what is dormant, and why. The immediate goal was narrow (get the website updated) but the conversations surfaced a lot more than a status list. Their insights from reviewing the matrix are worth a read.

This is also groundwork for a bigger project: updating the matrix so it can support portfolio strategy (what to prioritise, resource, pause, or close), shape the external narrative for funders and partners, and organise the mission internally around roles, dependencies, and resource flows.

What is changing

Eunsoo and Jason posted the proposed changes for comment through July. Arcs: 7 Gen Cities and Planetary Civics to be removed (Planetary Civics archived in Notion), M0 Cities renamed to X0 Economy, Regen Nutrition renamed to Resilient Food Systems. Labs: Societal Decisions and Beyond Labour to be removed, Sensing, Modeling & Mapping becomes the Risk Intelligence Lab, and Critical Geographies moves to hibernate.

The website updates in the week of 3 August. The Mission Pathways pop-up later in the month is a chance to pick the thread up again, and the new Dm Stewards will have a Mission & Pathways sub-group that could take ownership of the matrix as it evolves.

Review: Eunsoo, Jason  ·  Website: Gurden  ·  Input from across the team
Project highlights

What we're building

Sheffield: from civic hack to city structure

Annette has been invited to join Sheffield's Partnership Lab as a co-steward, which means our orchestration role in the city is no longer a civic hack but formalised into Sheffield's own structures. The Lab is a think-and-do team set up to drive collective action towards the Sheffield City Goals for 2035, modelled partly on work in Melbourne, and it has three years of funding secured to get started.

The stewarding group meets for a year, starting with an in-person onboarding in September. For Annette it is a route to bring what we have learned about many-to-many governance directly into how the Lab drives action across the city.

Co-steward: Annette  ·  Invitation from: Kate Josephs, Sheffield City Council

Civic Ledger week in Berlin

From 3 to 7 August, medulla hosted a week of Civic Ledger workshops, sessions, and conversations, with a public event co-hosted with Politics for Tomorrow, a presentation from Professor Kim, and a dinner for everyone at Markthalle Neun on Thursday evening.

Writing afterwards before heading to Seoul, Eunji and Fang said the team went in with a lot of open questions about civic tech development, data, collaboration models, and licensing, and the week opened those out into conversations and connections they are not trying to make sense of all at once yet. What they wanted to name straight away was the space itself, and the generosity people brought into it: holding space, making introductions, bringing expertise into the room, and handling logistics.

One of the sessions worked on operationalising the learning system and commitments through the Civic Ledger case, which is a useful bridge between the governance work and a live project. Conversation continues in #shared-civic-ledger.

Camden: a dinner, a workshop, and a food mission

On 5 August, forty people from across Camden and beyond sat down together at Calthorpe Community Garden for a Future Food Dinner. The following day they reconvened at the Crowndale Centre for the Camden Food Mission Workshop. Adam wrote up the journey that got us there, and it is worth reading in full.

Camden did not happen overnight. Indy had been seeding it for two or three years, speaking at Camden events and planting the idea of a Food Partnership long before we were commissioned, which is why the conditions there are some of the ripest we have had. Rose and Sue at Camden pushed behind the scenes to bring us in for this phase. The dinner and workshop were designed specifically to build and validate an indicative portfolio of future work, with the right people already behind it.

Adam was honest about the process too: a lot of ground-truthing with food experts across the UK and in Camden, real work building trust with partners on the ground, some relationships harder than others, and learning to navigate the scepticism that shows up when an organisation like ours enters the room. Sometimes you cannot change minds, but you can find common ground, and showing up with integrity usually builds trust in the end.

A genuinely beautiful dinner with top UK food system experts and people embedded in the work on the ground in Camden, brought to life by an incredible chef: cricket-flour bread, dishes made entirely from the community garden we sat in.

— Adam

The trust built over dinner carried straight into the next day, where the group self-organised around the areas they had most to contribute to. The ambition is already outgrowing the borough: other London boroughs are at the table, Camden looks likely to put more resources behind it, and the framing is shifting towards Food as Infrastructure as a London-wide initiative. Our investment committee has approved up to £50k to match further Camden investment, with the aim of converting £2–4m from other sources by Q1 next year. A National Lottery bid goes in this winter, and Professor Paul Behrens from Oxford, who was at both the dinner and the workshop, wants to partner on the work.

Lead: Adam  ·  Research and presentation: Aleksander  ·  Workshop design and facilitation: Siddhi  ·  Holding it together: Alizé  ·  Seeded by: Indy  ·  With: Camden Council, community food organisations, funders, and food system experts

A conversation worth joining

Sitting with the context

On 27 July Indy asked everyone to watch a video about the deep context our work sits inside, drawing on the actuarial Planetary Solvency report. The thread that followed was one of the more honest exchanges we have had in #org-hq.

Jack added a clear piece by the climate scientist Kevin Anderson on temperature and carbon budgets. Andrea, watching fires in Spain first-hand, said she found the predictions all too credible. Anette wrote from Sweden about how quickly heatwaves, drought, and the events in Ceuta are being turned into anti-democratic narrative. Olaf asked whether there is a word for being shocked by something you already knew.

For me the work being done at Dm is much more powerful and meaningful. But at least making people aware of these realities does help funders and institutions see the need to support our work.

— Andrea

Olaf has offered to host a space to talk this through and process it together, rather than each of us carrying it alone. If that would help you, tell him. He also flagged a short Nate Hagens video that he found kept him connected to hope, and said it made him look forward to the October gathering.

If this lands heavily

It is a lot to hold. Olaf's offer of a space to talk is open, and Indy suggested a watch-together session. Reach out to either of them, or to your Health Keepers, if you would rather not sit with it by yourself.
